# Training Plan Handoff Document

Context document for a health dashboard project. Covers the athlete's goals, constraints, full program, progression rules, and the reasoning behind design choices. Written August 2026.

---

## 1. Athlete Profile

- **Training experience:** Intermediate. Runs a structured 4-day split and progresses linearly on main lifts.
- **Key physical note: hypermobility.** This shapes several program rules (see Section 7). End range of motion is larger than it should be, so joints need muscles working under tension near (not at) end range. Passive stretching is out. Hard lockouts are out.
- **Schedule:** 4 gym sessions per week (2 upper, 2 lower; each has a heavy and a light day). Off days are home-only, capped at ~20 minutes, and must be low intensity because the athlete is usually sore.
- **Home equipment:** one 25 lb kettlebell, one 35 lb kettlebell, a yoga mat, one weak resistance band. No gym visits on off days.
- **Gym equipment notes:** leg curls are done on a lying machine (only one available). Squats are on a Smith machine. Sled is available and liked.
- **Preference:** no exercise gets more than 2 sets, except bench press. Two sets is easier to lock in mentally, so both sets must be treated as real work sets.

## 2. Goals and Deadline

Deadline: **end of December 2026** (about 5 months from program start in early August).

| Goal | Baseline (Aug 2026) | Target | On pace? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trap bar deadlift | 260 lbs x 5 | 300 lbs x 5 | Yes (~2 lbs/week) |
| Smith machine squat | 185 lbs x 5 | 225 lbs x 5 | Yes (~2 lbs/week) |
| Bench press | 165 lbs x 5 | 225 lbs x 5 | **Stretch goal.** 195–205 x 5 by December is realistic; 225 likely lands in spring. |
| Pullups | 7 bodyweight reps | 10 bodyweight reps | Yes |
| Dips | 10 bodyweight reps | 10 reps | **Already achieved.** Maintain. |
| Vertical jump | — | In-game dunk | Skill + power goal; progress tracked by approach jump attempts |

Two goals trade off: gaining bodyweight helps the bench and hurts the dunk. The athlete should decide which goal owns the fall. Food and sleep will decide most of the bench outcome.

## 3. The Program

Days are slotted into the week by the athlete. Only two scheduling rules:

1. Don't stack heavy lower right after light lower.
2. Give bench a day or two between sessions.

### Stretch Under Tension Day (~20 min, home)

Loaded stretching suits hypermobility: the muscle under tension protects the joint, which is why this is fine while passive stretching is not. Rules: move slow, stop at the **first real stretch** (never max range — the athlete's end range is past where it should be), and never push into sharp or pinchy joint sensations (that means the stretch has drifted past muscle range into joint range; back off an inch).

| Exercise | Dose | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Goblet squat pry (25 lb) | 2 x 45 sec | Hips + ankles for squatting |
| Long lunge hold | 2 x 30 sec/side | Hip flexors, for jumping. Add the bell if easy. |
| Slow single-leg RDL (35 lb) | 5/side, 5 sec down | Hamstrings for the deadlift. Pause at bottom stretch. |
| Bent-knee calf stretch on step | 2 x 8/side, 3 sec pause | Achilles for the jump |
| Kettlebell pullover (25 lb) | 2 x 8, 3 sec down | Lats + chest — hits pullups, bench, and dips at once |
| Deep pushup hold | 2 x 15–20 sec | Bottom of bench and dips. Kneel if needed. |
| Prying floor press stretch | 20 sec/side | Chest opener |

Short-on-time version: pullover, goblet pry, calf stretch.

If wrecked: walk 30 minutes instead. Recovery beats one more session.

## 4. Progression and Cycle Rules

- **Main lifts (deadlift, squat, bench heavy day):** add 2.5–5 lbs whenever all work sets move well. Target pace on deadlift and squat is ~2 lbs/week.
- **Pullups:** progress through frequency, not grinding. Most sets stay at 4–5 clean reps; an all-out test set every few weeks tracks the path to 10. Optional: add 2 easy pullup sets at the start of lower days if elbows feel good.
- **Deload:** every 4–5 weeks, take a lighter week — same exercises, weight dropped 10–15%. Joints need it more than most people's given the hypermobility, and the bar usually moves faster the week after.
- **Basketball:** counts as fatigued jump work. On days the athlete plays, drop that day's off-day session.
- **November option:** once the base is built, a few jumps at the end of one session per week can practice expressing power while tired. Finishing touch, not main work.

## 5. Design Rationale (why the plan looks this way)

These points came out of back-and-forth and should be preserved as reasoning, not just rules:

- **Jumps moved to the start of lower days.** The old routine had 2x10 weighted jumps superset with 2x10 depth jumps at the end, after deadlifts, squats, and machine work. Jumping tired teaches slow mechanics, and tired reps don't raise the vertical. Low-dose fresh jumping (2x4 on heavy day) primes the nervous system rather than draining it — fatigue comes from volume, and the volume was the actual problem. Total jump volume dropped by more than half, but every rep is now done fresh, which is the only kind that raises a vertical.
- **Why not train jumping fatigued for in-game dunking?** Fresh training raises the ceiling, and the ceiling is what carries into games. If a dunk needs a 32-inch jump and the fresh max is 33, the in-game (tired) jump is ~29 — rim grabs. Raise the fresh max to 37 and the tired jump is ~33 — dunks. Fatigued athleticism comes free from playing basketball.
- **Depth jump dose cut hard.** They're the most demanding jump variant. Past ~5 reps a set, quality collapses and the athlete is just absorbing landings, which is where injury risk sits — especially with hypermobility.
- **Bench is the volume exception.** 2x5 twice a week cannot move a bench from 165 to 225. Heavy day went to 3–4x5, light day to 3x8, plus pushups at home. The bottleneck was total flat volume, not a missing angle — which is why **incline was rejected**: it carries over less than rep-for-rep, and dips plus pushups already give pressing variety. If flat sets feel stale around October, one light-day set can become incline at 8 reps. *(Revised August 5, 2026: incline pressed on the heavy day now counts toward the 3–4×5 prescription — the plan check pools incline sets with flat bench instead of flagging them as extra. The original reasoning above stands for programming purposes; this is about how a logged session is scored.)*
- **Cuts made:** cable flies off heavy day (third or fourth exercise hitting the same tissue as bench + dips + pushups), one of row/pulldown off heavy day, and leg extensions entirely. The routine wasn't bloated, it was spread thin — cuts freed time and energy for the lifts being chased.
- **Kept on purpose:** preacher curls (elbow protection under rising pullup volume), Y raises (shoulder health), leg curls (hamstrings drive deadlift and jump), and the sled. **The sled stays** — the athlete found it helped a lot as a warmup, and it earns its spot: full-body blood flow with no eccentric, so no added soreness, plus free work capacity.
- **The 3x3 / 2x4 heavy-day jumps are plain jumps.** This was a point of confusion once: they are not weighted, not depth jumps, not a superset. Bodyweight, max intent, full rest.
- **Never superset explosive work.** Every rep must be fresh and fast; supersetting defeats the purpose.

## 6. Open Item: the Ankle Drill

The athlete swapped single-leg calf raises for the **knee-to-wall drill** (a.k.a. weightbearing dorsiflexion lunge). Flag raised: knee-to-wall trains *range*, and the calf raises trained *strength* — different jobs, and with hypermobility, range is usually the thing already in surplus.

**Test:** stand facing a wall, foot about 4 inches away, drive the knee over the toes without the heel lifting.
- Touches the wall easily → ankles aren't stiff; the drill won't do much. Skip it.
- Can't touch → do it, 2 x 10 slow per side.

Either way, the two-leg loaded calf raise stays in the Off Day A slot, because the Achilles is a big part of the jump and pogos alone only cover part of it. Tibialis raises were dropped by the athlete's choice.

## 7. Hypermobility Rules (apply everywhere)

1. Stop just short of full lockout on bench, dips, and any pressing.
2. On bench: shoulder blades pinned; don't sink the bar so deep the shoulders roll forward. If the front of the shoulder starts complaining as volume climbs, this bottom position is the first thing to check.
3. No long passive stretching. Loaded/tension stretching only.
4. In stretching: stop at the first real stretch, never max range. Sharp or pinchy joint sensation = drifted past muscle into joint range; back off.
5. Controlled tempos throughout.
6. Carries, overhead holds, and Copenhagen planks double as joint-stability work.
7. Deload weeks matter more than they would for most lifters.

## 8. What a Dashboard Should Track

Suggestions for the health dashboard this document feeds:

- **Weekly working weights** on trap bar deadlift, Smith squat, and bench (heavy-day top sets), plotted against the linear pace lines: deadlift 260→300, squat 185→225 (both ~2 lbs/week through December), bench 165→~200 by December.
- **Pullup test-set reps** (the periodic all-out set), path from 7 → 10.
- **Jump quality, not just volume** — e.g., a per-session note on whether depth-jump reps stayed fast, and best approach-jump result at the rim (rim grab → hang → dunk attempt milestones).
- **Deload weeks** on the calendar every 4–5 weeks, with a check that they actually happened.
- **Off-day compliance** (A / B / stretch day / walk / basketball), since basketball substitutes for that day's session.
- **Bodyweight**, because of the bench-vs-dunk tradeoff — the athlete should see the trend and decide which goal owns the fall.
- **Soreness or joint complaints**, especially front-of-shoulder on bench and anything sharp at a joint, since those trigger the hypermobility rules above.
- **Bench reality check:** flag around late October whether the pace supports 225x5 by December or the realistic 195–205, so expectations adjust early instead of in December.
